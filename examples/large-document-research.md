# Large-document research: bounded clauses and exceptions

Illustrative fixture, not legal advice or a claim about an actual contract.

**Request:** “Find the termination notice period and exceptions in the 400-page agreement; use no more than 1,500 retrieved tokens.”

Allocate about 1,000 tokens to notebook retrieval and 500 to original verification. Identify the exact agreement/version before extraction. Decompose into notice period, applicability, and exceptions. Ask for relevant clauses and cross-references in a single bounded response when provenance remains separate; avoid a chapter-by-chapter summary.

Fixture notebook response: “30 days, section 9.2,” omitting exceptions. Use at most one corrective query to request cross-referenced exceptions. Inspect the relevant original section and its referenced exception clause within the remaining budget. Confirm units, calendar/business days and the conditions that trigger the period. If the PDF page label differs from the viewer page index, record both only when known.

Suppose the inspected original version says 30 calendar days for ordinary termination and immediate termination for a defined breach after an unmet cure condition. Return both conditions with their verified section locators. If a newer amendment is discovered, verify applicability before treating it as controlling. A newer unrelated draft does not automatically supersede the signed agreement.

If the relevant exception cannot be obtained within budget, report the supported notice rule and explicitly leave exceptions incomplete. Do not say “there are no exceptions” because retrieval omitted them. Scanned tables, footnotes and OCR errors may require the original page image; text extraction alone may not preserve them.

**Pass criteria:** correct version selected; no whole-document dump; exception and units checked; unresolved coverage disclosed; fallback counts toward the same budget; absence limited to inspected scope.
